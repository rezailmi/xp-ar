# Why this sketch exists

Emil Kowalski’s note (the one Reza sent): a prototype is useful because you can touch the real thing. Its flaw is that it looks finished, so people treat every choice as decided. Building is cheap. Write the why first. Align. Then prototype. Taste over speed.

This file is the why. The page is a sketch after it, not a product.

## Why we are making this

An AR toy where your agent is **in the house with you**. You walk into a living room. Someone is already on the rug. You talk.

Not a dashboard. Not Handmade. Not a second Windows XP desktop. Not a chat app with a 3D skin.

The point of putting it in a browser is so Reza can stand in that feeling for thirty seconds — not to ship an OS, a headset app, or an assistant brand.

## Who

Reza. A 30-second click-through. No account. No backend. No demo script.

## One job

Feel *this character appeared in my living room* — then talk to it.

If that lands, the sketch did its job. If the room feels like a game level, or the UI feels like a shipped window, it failed.

## Locked (Reza said)

- **Place:** a domestic living room.
- **Body:** a PlayStation-era mascot. Crash *energy*, not likeness. The name is **Pips**. Not Rover. Not a dog.
- **Talk:** a Clippy-style paper balloon. Type in it. The reply replaces the copy.

## Still open

Label these so the prototype does not fake consensus.

| Question | What this sketch does | What is not decided |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is Clippy the long-term talk model? | One paper balloon, this pass only | Whether talk stays a balloon, becomes voice, or something else |
| Headset later, or browser-only forever? | Browser. No WebXR. | Whether this ever leaves the tab |
| Live agent, or canned lines? | Canned replies. v0. | Whether a real model ever sits in the balloon |

Do not read the finished pixels as answers to those three.

## Taste spend

One room. One body. One paper balloon.

Spend the budget on: a room you could actually stand in, a mascot that reads as a house guest, a balloon that feels taped to his head.

## Refuse

Bliss yard leftovers. Rover. Luna XP windows. Inter. Purple gradients. Nested cards. Hype copy. Crash IP. A UI that looks like a shipped product.

The on-screen line **Sketch. Decisions still open.** is there so the look cannot pretend alignment already happened.
